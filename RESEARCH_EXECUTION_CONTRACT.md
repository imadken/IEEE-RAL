A. Fixed project objective
Project: The Double Effect of Diagnostic Probing: Separating Information and Physical Intervention in Robot Failure Recovery (DUAL-REC).

Research question: For an ambiguous manipulation failure, what does a diagnostic physical action contribute through (i) information that changes the subsequent recovery choice and (ii) the physical state change the action itself induces? Which conventional probe-selection policies make poor choices when these effects disagree, particularly with uncertain probe dynamics?

Target scientific contribution: a controlled, reproducible physical-intervention study and, only if demonstrated, a computationally useful state-aware recovery-selection method. The information/physical-effect decomposition is standard decision theory, NOT a novel theorem. A strong state-aware robust planner is a required comparator. A negative or null result is scientifically valid; it is not automatically an IROS/RA-L paper.

No result is precommitted. Never manufacture a fault regime to force an advantage over baselines.

D. Pre-registered scientific definitions
Let a failed history h have an unobserved physical mechanism theta and observable task state o. Candidate direct recoveries are r in R; diagnostic actions are p in P. The same initial failed physical state must be reconstructed independently for every candidate intervention. Recoveries must have a fixed, task-appropriate horizon and common costs, and must be compared on normalized utility measured from actual simulator outcomes. A success label is an engineering metric, not a hidden-cause input.

For a fixed probe p, define:
- V0: best no-probe recovery value from failed state, within the fixed budget.
- VB(p): best recovery value after executing p when the subsequent recovery policy is NOT allowed to condition on its diagnostic measurement, minus probe cost; it may use only the explicitly predeclared low-level safety/control signals that all conditions use.
- VA(p): best value after p when the recovery policy may condition on the diagnostic measurement, minus probe cost.
VA(p)-V0 = [VA(p)-VB(p)] + [VB(p)-V0]. This is an exact bookkeeping identity, not a novel theorem. The two bracketed terms are respectively the value of the specified additional information and the physical-plus-cost effect under this precise policy information restriction. Do not call them natural causal direct/indirect effects without separately proving the required causal assumptions. Do not allow post-probe outcome, hidden mass/friction, model ground truth, or the optimal response label to leak into the information-masked policy.

Hypotheses to test, NOT assume:
- H1: some physically realistic failure mechanisms produce overlapping passive histories but favor different recovery actions.
- H2: for at least one physical probe, information benefit and physical-plus-cost effect have materially different signs or magnitudes; some probes are informative but harmful, or weakly informative but physically helpful.
- H3: under some independently specified errors in probe-transition models, a probe selector that ignores physical side effects has worse recovery utility than a selector that models them, while a correctly specified robust planner may already perform optimally.
H1 is the ONLY hypothesis qualified in Days 5 to 7. H2 and H3 are not authorized for implementation yet.
