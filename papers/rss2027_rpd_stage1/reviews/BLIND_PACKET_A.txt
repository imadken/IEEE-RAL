Title: When Does Physical Diagnosis Compromise Robot Recovery?

Abstract:

Probing a jammed peg can reveal its misalignment and also move it beyond the corrections the robot can execute. How often does diagnosis cause this loss beyond waiting, and does accounting for it improve completion? We define recovery opportunity as the set of fixed feedback policies that achieve a declared goal within the remaining time. A matched physical probe--hold protocol measures individual-option loss and entire-repertoire erasure at a common deadline. We specify a simulation-only assembly study with geometric perturbations, native control, proprioceptive responses, and two corrective policies. H1 tests two population properties separately: at least 10\% compliant repertoire erasure under a frozen information-seeking selector, and positive predictive diagnostic signal. A secondary weighted-score test checks signal on erasure cases. H2 tests a five-percentage-point completion benefit over constrained information seeking, with matched task-aware controls. A constructed example establishes possibility, not physical prevalence. The contribution is the recovery-specific measurement and validation methodology; its contingent-planning formulation is established, and assembly validation remains prospective.
