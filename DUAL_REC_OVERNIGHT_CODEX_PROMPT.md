F. Branch A: information-versus-physical-effect pilot (only after qualifying D7B)
Central estimand: Given failed state/history h and a fixed physical probe p, compare (0) recovery without probe, (1) perform probe and choose recovery without using its newly acquired diagnostic information, and (2) perform the exact same probe but adapt the high-level recovery selection to its observation. Low-level feedback required to safely execute each selected recovery remains enabled in both arms. Thus, adaptive - blind measures the decision value of the probe observation, and blind - direct measures its physical effect plus probe cost, under a fixed initial failure distribution. This is established decision theory, not a new theorem or demonstrated advantage.

Pre-register at most two physical probes before observing their effects, chosen from safe, brief gripper-force/closure testing and a short lift-hold or reposition test, depending on the physically qualified failure mechanisms. Explicitly document exactly how a probe changes gripper contacts, pose, effort, and available future recovery budget. Use identical probe action sequences for blind and adaptive arms on matched reconstructed states. For blind, keep high-level recovery independent of new probe measurements, though standard low-level closed-loop control remains available. For adaptive, use only measured probe observations and a model trained exclusively on development data.

Compare direct recovery, probe-blind, probe-adaptive, a baseline information-gain probe policy, and a simple nominal decision-aware planner with matched action candidates, observations and cost. If tractable, implement an exact finite-state reduced model for correctness; do not claim superiority over a Bayes-optimal planner given the true model. Separate model-estimation and evaluation data. Use no simulator hidden parameters in policy execution. Outcome metrics: recovery success, net utility, probe time, negative physical effect frequency, decision regret relative to an evaluation-only oracle, and difference of information/physical components with paired CIs. The pilot should include at least one candidate where the probe changes the physical state measurably; do not manually label it beneficial or harmful.

Use at most 24 additional fresh development base seeds and 16 independent held-out base seeds, disjoint from all earlier qualification seeds; preregister both exact seed ranges before execution and cap the intervention pilot at 1,600 physical continuation branches.
