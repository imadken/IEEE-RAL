      When Does Diagnostic Interaction Compromise
                  Robot Recovery?

   Abstract—A diagnostic action changes both what an au-
tonomous robot knows and which task continuations remain pos-
sible. An informative contact may preserve conventional motion
constraints yet remove an otherwise executable correction. We
study this coupling in physics-based robotic simulation through
post-diagnosis recoverability, the set of fixed feedback corrections
that can complete the task within the remaining deadline. Our
methodology combines a physical belief update, a response-
contingent recovery decision, and matched probe-versus-hold
interventions that distinguish contact-induced option loss from
elapsed time. We formulate two quantitative hypotheses: in-

   A constructed finite-world sweep varies the intensity of
this interaction (Table II). As intensity increases, its predicted
diagnostic information remains useful while the fine probe
causes more unrecoverable continuations. The task-aware pol-
icy chooses the coarse probe in the affected worlds, and a
general contingent planner with the same terminal objective
makes identical choices. The sweep is analytical evidence of
a mechanism and its decision consequence, not a measured
SAPIEN dose response or an H1 population estimate.
   What makes the hypotheses informative? H1 predicts
more than a single harmful contact: within the fixed source
distribution, IG-selected diagnosis must erase an entire hold-
retained correction repertoire often enough to matter, and a
majority of those cases must exhibit a material true-mode
signal. The underlying physical quantities qp for each fixed
probe and h clarify the mechanism. A large qp with small
qIG indicates that the selector avoids disruptive probes; small
qp despite high probe-selection frequency indicates that this
regime preserves recovery. Neither can be inferred from the
single aggregate rate alone. The three fixed amplitudes and
predecision contact-mode strata assess where the interaction is
consequential, without selecting a favorable evaluation subset.
