       When Does Evidence Justify Robot Recovery?
                                       Author Names Omitted for Anonymous Review


   Abstract—A recovery skill can rescue a failed execution,
disrupt one that would succeed, or fail along with continua-
tion. Historical success alone does not distinguish these cases,
especially when observations are incomplete and each logged
execution records only one action. We formulate Decision-
Sufﬁcient Recovery Explanations (DSRE): minimum observ-
able facts supporting a named recovery obligation within an
explicitly calibrated region and intervention evidence source.
The method combines source-dependent outcome uncertainty
with independent statistical qualiﬁcation, returning a certiﬁcate
or a constructive account of missing evidence. Two simulated
manipulation studies use randomized single-action learning data
and isolated paired evaluation. On independent test cohorts,
qualiﬁed selection completes 227/256 grasps and 253/256 pushes,
with 84 and 126 necessary rescues and no observed binary task
harms. Matched exact rules, direct partial-identiﬁcation rules and
trees make identical decisions. The formulation explains when
marginal evidence sufﬁces, when it cannot identify harm, and
why a short explanation must preserve its calibrated scope.
