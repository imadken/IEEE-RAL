When Does Physical Diagnosis Compromise Robot
                  Recovery?
                                       Author Names Omitted for Anonymous Review


   Abstract—A diagnostic contact can reveal why a manipulation
failed while destroying an otherwise available correction. How
frequently does this happen beyond the effect of waiting, and
does anticipating it improve recovery? We study post-diagnosis
recoverability: which fixed feedback corrections still complete
the task within a common deadline. Matched probe and equal-
duration hold executions separate changes caused by contact
from lost time, while controlled diagnostic-response tests measure
information gained. We formulate two falsifiable hypotheses
for contact-rich assembly. H1 predicts at least 10% constraint-
compliant loss of the entire declared recovery repertoire under
a frozen information-seeking policy, together with positive diag-
nostic signal; a secondary test examines their co-occurrence. H2
predicts at least five percentage points higher task completion
for recoverability-aware diagnosis under matched constraints
and observations. We provide exact finite-model analysis, a fully
specified simulation protocol, and an interpretation of positive
and null outcomes. The physical hypotheses remain prospective;
the contribution is a recovery-specific measurement and decision
methodology, not a new contingent-planning solver.

A. What the controls can and cannot identify
   Equal-duration holding controls elapsed time under the
same feedback law; it does not make a probe non-disturbing.
The common root-frozen estimator prevents a poorer post-
probe belief from silently redefining recovery commands.
Differences still concern these fixed feedback policies and
their interaction with contact and controller state, not all
