Proposed title: When Does Physical Diagnosis Destroy Recovery Opportunities?
1. Scientific thesis and novelty boundary
A robot performing active diagnosis does not merely observe a fixed state. Diagnostic contact changes the state and can alter which subsequent corrective actions can still succeed. Ordinary geometric safety, information gain, and terminal task recoverability are different quantities. The research question is:
When does a diagnostic action preserve ordinary state-safety constraints yet eliminate an otherwise available task-recovery option, and how should this change task-aware diagnosis?

This phenomenon is meaningful to manipulation, contact-sensitive assembly, and active fault diagnosis. Prior work already recognizes that contact sensing disturbs scenes, that information gathering and physical safety can conflict, that task-aware tactile probing is possible, and that POMDPs can plan under uncertainty. Our methodological target is therefore specific, not a first-ever claim about physical information or novel planning: a matched, source-qualified definition and measurement of diagnostic probe-induced loss of future task recovery, separated from equal-duration waiting, and a decision criterion based on that quantity.

The work uses established strong/POMDP planning as a positive control. The paired simulator branches exist only in offline evaluation and are not observable during deployment.

Closest positioning, verified primary sources:
- s-FEAST, Science Robotics 2024, https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.adn4722: active fault diagnosis with probabilistic physical-state safety. In principle task viability can be formulated in this planner, so do not claim the planner cannot express it. We ask a different empirical measurement question.
- DA-GRD, 2026 preprint, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29065: tactile probes improving task-relevant grasp agreement. It too changes physical state; our specific measured object is subsequent recovery-option erosion relative to a time-matched no-probe control.
- Active perception via manipulation, Springer Handbook of Robotics, 2016: contact sensing disturbing the scene is long understood; do not claim that disturbance itself is new.
- Contingent and reach-avoid belief planning are standard computational tools, not claimed inventions.
2. One methodology and exact formal core
At a qualified robot checkpoint s, fix a finite action catalogue A of executable recovery macros, a task-success predicate U, and a common post-checkpoint deadline tau. For a physical diagnostic probe p of duration d, let T_p^d(s) be its actual post-probe state. Let H_d(s) be the post-state from an equal-duration physical neutral hold, with arm feedback, contact, gravity and task time still advancing. For any corresponding state x, define:
K_tau(x) = {a in A : terminal task success U_tau(x,a)=1}.
The primary recovery-option erasure outcome on one source-qualified parent checkpoint is:
L_p(s) = 1[K_tau(H_d(s)) != empty AND K_tau(T_p^d(s)) = empty].
Also report option creation, per-action loss/gain, complete recoveries, and whether geometric state allowances hold along actual probe/hold paths. Define the joint event of erasure and sampled allowed-state compliance, rather than restricting the analysis after observing a treatment-dependent safety outcome. The estimand is the population probability of that joint event on the specified eligible checkpoint distribution. Repeat branches within a parent do NOT become independent samples.

Measured probe response Y_p comes only from declared permissible online sensors, with pre-probe history X and evaluator-only diagnostic target Z. Quantify incremental held-out prediction of Z from (X,Y_p) against X alone, rather than claiming mutual information from a tiny pilot. With a learned or known transition model, compare information-seeking, one-step task-consensus, and full-model planning at equal source access and time/actuation budgets. The task-aware planner can use an estimate of lost recovery options and task gain; it is not advertised as a new planner class.

Source assumptions: matched reproducible initial checkpoint; physically executed probes/hold; actual simulator terminal outcomes; fixed action repertoire and horizon; source parity; missingness and native crashes explicitly counted; no source outcome inferred from command intent; simulator-only safety/terminal semantics. Guarantees are conditional on source qualification and task catalogue, not real-robot guarantees.
3. Two central hypotheses and falsification criteria
H1: Diagnostic probes can cause task-recovery-option erasure not explained by equal elapsed time, even while sampled geometric bounds remain satisfied.

Test by measuring the joint event of option erasure, time-matched hold viability, and within-sample geometric allowance on independent held-out checkpoints; count actual probe contacts, whether objects move, and source-missing outcomes. A null result can bound the frequency of this phenomenon on the specified contact distribution. One analytical three-world demonstration illustrates possibility, not prevalence.

H2: In the regimes where probe-induced erasure is material and probe responses are informative, explicitly accounting for remaining recovery options changes the achievable completion-versus-information tradeoff compared with choosing probes only for information gain.

Test with frozen information-only, consensus, task-aware and full-model positive-control planners, common sensors and probe menus, and held-out parents. If policies tie, information-only is already sufficient for that regime, or the probe model is not informative, report that diagnostic conclusion. Do not claim a novel POMDP solver or a guaranteed action-selection advantage.

The primary experiment measures H1. H2 is secondary and requires a qualified, informative sensor interface. This hierarchy makes a negative H1 result scientifically interpretable without inventing positive H2 results.
4. Allowed Stage 1 preliminary evidence
- An exact, constructed three-world demonstration where the most informative probe removes a recovery option while a coarser probe preserves it. Explicitly label analytical illustration, not robot evidence.
- The present fixed 10-parent-per-task RPD pilot returned 38 native traces for each of PickCube and PushCube, showing actual actuator execution and qualified contact effects in a subset, but both workers crashed on the 39th planned branch, before neutral-hold outcomes. This supports only technical interaction and a source blocker. It does NOT estimate probe-induced recovery-option erasure.
- DSRE's previous positive grasping/pushing recovery selection results are not RPD probe outcomes. Do NOT use them to claim RPD physical viability.
The RSS Stage 1 abstract can be methodological with zero claimed RPD physical erasure events, because the problem, method, and falsifiable validation are fully specified. Do not hide known relevant method equivalences or rewrite source failures as normal negative physical results.

6. Feasible Stage 2 charter proposal to state at the end of the paper
The essential primary extension is one independently qualified contact-sensitive task family, with the second family as a controlled replication if its source qualifies. The chosen fixed components are: two ten-tick diagnostic probes, ten-tick physical neutral hold, unchanged continue/recover 70-tick terminal macros, common 90-tick post-checkpoint deadline, fixed observation channels and terminal predicate. No new learned foundation model or hardware is needed.
1. Source qualification before science evaluation: isolate each physics branch in a fresh process or bounded batch after a fixed source-equivalence audit. Investigate the two native SIGSEGVs observed on branch 39 (the first recover branch after two repeats of the second probe type). Preserve the 38 complete prior traces and all aborted outcomes. Predeclare the eventual one-probe-versus-hold protocol, which does not require length-two probe histories. Measure startup/throughput and fail safely if native source cannot qualify. This is engineering feasibility, not a result supporting H1.
2. Discovery study: 64 independently sampled eligible parents for a task, frozen probes and failure/checkpoint conditions. Determine usable contact/observation coverage, baseline option-set balance, sample precision and whether all actions genuinely execute. Freeze the analysis and relevant sample-size assumptions before confirmation.
3. Primary matched confirmation: 256 new independent eligible parents per qualified task; six terminal branches per parent (two probes plus matched hold, each followed by both fixed recovery macros), for 1,536 terminal evaluations per task, plus any separately predeclared replay audits. One parent supplies one independent clustered record. Compare erasure, option creation, individual-option loss, path allowance, contacts and costs. Report original-seed attrition and sensitivity bounds; no replacement after reading outcomes.
4. Analysis: prespecified per-probe paired erasure proportions with independent-parent exact confidence intervals and multiplicity control; binary terminal task outcomes; observation information measured with a discovery-frozen decoder and held-out parents. A zero-event frequency over 256 eligible independent parents has a one-sided 95% exact upper bound about 1.16% for a single predeclared event, so a well-qualified null can still be informative. Do not transfer that bound across multiple probes without multiplicity allocation.
5. Policy consequence, secondary: compare information-only selection, task consensus, and full-model task-aware planning with common models/sensors and matched action/time costs. Assess whether the observed erasure regime changes achieved task completion, not whether the new method beats a deliberately disadvantaged planner. If H1 is absent or sensor responses carry no diagnostic information, explain the limitation rather than manufacture a policy effect.
Practical schedule: source isolation and feasibility measurement first, then discovery, then confirmation and audit. Existing exact planning and physics measurement harnesses reduce implementation work, but source stability is currently unverified and this plan cannot honestly guarantee completion in a few days. 8. Manuscript-ready prose draft (review and revise before submission)
Abstract candidate
Robots often diagnose manipulation failures through physical interaction. Yet a probe that makes a failure easier to understand can also alter the object or contact state and eliminate a corrective action that would otherwise succeed. Existing active-sensing approaches study information acquisition and physical constraints, but task-recovery feasibility is a separate quantity that should be measured explicitly. We ask when diagnostic contact consumes recovery opportunities without violating the declared geometric allowance. We define the recovery-option set over a fixed action repertoire and terminal horizon, and propose a matched intervention protocol: from the same qualified checkpoint, compare each probe against an equal-duration neutral hold, then execute the same recovery alternatives in isolated evaluation branches. This identifies probe-associated option loss separately from elapsed-time effects. The framework also specifies how such evidence should enter task-aware probe selection, with information gain, task consensus and full-model belief planning as controls. A constructed example demonstrates that greater information can coexist with fewer remaining recovery options. A preregistered manipulation study will measure the physical frequency, mechanism and task consequences of this divergence, including informative null outcomes.
Concise end-of-paper Stage 2 paragraph
To test the hypotheses, we will first source-qualify fixed contact probes and matched neutral holds, then measure their effects on the terminal recoverability of a fixed continue/recover repertoire. Each qualified task uses a 64-parent discovery cohort to freeze sensing, contact, analysis and model choices, followed by 256 independent held-out parents. Two ten-step probes and one ten-step hold are each followed by both fixed terminal macros from separately replayed identical checkpoints, totaling 1,536 matched terminal evaluations per task. The primary endpoint is the parent-level frequency with which a probe eliminates all recoveries available after holding, while maintaining the registered sampled geometric allowance. We will separately assess lost individual actions, observed contact, task completion and missing outcomes. A secondary evaluation compares information gain, task consensus and full-model task-aware diagnosis under matched information and actuation budgets. This design can quantify a positive effect or bound its absence, while separating physical probe effects from time alone.
